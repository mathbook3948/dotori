# dotori

## Overview

dotori is one person's assistant that lives on their PC. Its windows should feel calm, soft and quick. The owner talks to the assistant the way they talk to anyone in a messenger: each side speaks in bubbles, the assistant on the left with its picture and the owner on the right.

Smoothness matters most. Nothing jumps, nothing flickers, and the window never waits on the network to answer a click or a key.

This file is the design the windows are moving to. Until a window has moved, `docs/architecture/windows.md` and the code describe how it is now, and a small fix follows them. A change that moves a window to this design updates `windows.md` in the same pull request.

## Colors

- The owner picks a theme in the settings window and every window changes at once. Each theme has a light and a dark set, chosen by the system look or by the owner.
- The themes are acorn, graphite, paper, forest and indigo. Acorn is the default: a warm off-white or a warm charcoal canvas, white or dark cards, and an amber `primary`.
- `canvas` is the window background. `surface` is anything that sits on it, such as a card or a button. A field on a card takes `canvas`. `raised` is a window's top bar and the composer area.
- `bubble` is the owner's message. It is a neutral shade of the canvas, never `primary`.
- The main button is the `text` color filled in, with `canvas` words: warm black in the light look and warm white in the dark. Each screen has at most one, for the action the owner most likely takes next.
- `primary` colors small marks only: a step done in the onboarding, the check by a chosen item, list bullets and a focused field's line. A mark drawn on `primary` would not stand out in every theme, so a filled mark such as a ticked checkbox takes the main button's colors. It never fills a large area or a button, and it is never text.
- `selection` is the background of selected text.
- `ok`, `warn` and `danger` say a result worked, waits for the owner, or failed. Each has a `-soft` fill for a card that carries that meaning. They are used as words, icons and fills, never as dots.
- Every color used for text reaches 4.5:1 against every background it sits on, placeholders included.
- An icon or a mark that carries meaning reaches 3:1 against what it sits on, such as `feature.icon` on each `feature` tile.
- The logo keeps its own colors, `logo.light` on a light theme and `logo.dark` on a dark one.

## Typography

- The windows write in Kakao Small Sans, bundled with the app under the SIL Open Font License, with its ligatures off. Code uses the fixed-width font each OS has.
- Kakao Small Sans has a regular and a bold weight, so a `fontWeight` of 600 draws bold.
- dotori's replies use `reply`, everything else in the conversation uses `body`, and labels and notes use `small` or `caption`. Hints under fields use `hint`.
- Headings in replies and window titles inside a window use `title`. The greeting of the onboarding window uses `display` and its other screens use `heading`. Headings are never colored.
- Korean text breaks only between words.
- Bold marks a few words that matter, such as the time of an event.

## Layout

- Every window opens at `size.window` and can shrink down to `size.window-min`. Every window has minimize, maximize and close buttons on the right of its strip, and a double click on the strip fills the screen with the window or brings it back. While the window fills the screen, the maximize button shows two squares and the edges no longer resize it.
- The conversation and the settings share one main window. On the left of the conversation, a side panel `size.sidebar` wide on the canvas lists the routines and the notes on `surface` rows, each group under a `muted` heading that opens its part of the settings. The gear in the strip shows the settings in the same window, in place of the side panel and the conversation. The gear hides while the settings show, and the settings' own way back returns to the conversation.
- What is read stays at a readable width however wide the window is: the conversation and the composer sit in a column of at most `size.column` in the middle of the chat window, and each onboarding step in a column of `size.walk` in the middle of its window.
- The chat window is the conversation and the composer, with no header and no title bar of the OS where dotori can draw its own. A quiet strip at the top holds the settings button on the left and the window buttons on the right, and the owner drags the window by it. The strip has no fill of its own: the conversation and the side panel scroll on under it, and only its buttons float over them. Over its height the canvas fades from solid to clear, so what passes under it fades out before it reaches the buttons. A press on the strip never reaches what lies under it. On macOS the system's window buttons stay on the left and the settings button moves to the right end of the strip, clear of them. Where the OS still draws its own title bar, the close button is left to it. Replies carry no picture: the owner talks to one assistant, and its picture shows on the splash, the empty chat, the settings and the taskbar.
- The settings keep the main window's strip. Under it, a list of sections `size.sidebar` wide sits on the canvas on the left, where the side panel was: first 대화 with a `muted` arrow, which shows the conversation again, then 일반, 대화 모델, 연결, 메모, 루틴, 여러 PC and 안전, each with a `muted` icon. The chosen section takes `bubble` and bold words, and the pointer lights the others with `bubble`.
- The chosen section fills a `surface` pane on the right with a `rounded.xl` top left corner. Its parts sit one under another with no card around them, divided by a `line`, and a field on the pane takes `canvas`.
- The settings window has no save button. A choice saves when it is made, and a field saves when the owner leaves it or closes the window, only if it changed. A save says nothing, since dotori uses the change at once and nothing is left for the owner to do. Only a failure shows, in one line at the bottom of the pane, unless it is a field problem, which shows by its fields.
- The onboarding window opens on the first run and again until the owner finishes it, at the step they left. It greets the owner on the first screen, then walks three steps: the API key, the name and theme, and the other places to talk. The greeting is left aligned like the rest, never a centered hero, and shows what to ask as a short conversation rather than how setup works. The key step asks only for what dotori cannot start without: the key for Anthropic, and the address, model and key for an OpenAI-compatible model.
- The strip shows no status. The chat window says nothing while things work. While dotori starts, the chat window shows a splash in place of the conversation and the composer: an empty strip with no buttons, and the assistant's picture and name as the owner set them in the middle, with no other words. Only when dotori could not start does a line under the picture say so and what to do, and the strip's buttons come back so the owner can close the window. When dotori cannot be reached after the window reached it, one quiet line under the composer, where the Enter hint is, says so. It never names which PC answers. If dotori stops after the window saw it running, the window closes, because dotori only stops when the owner quits it or it crashes, and it never restarts itself.
- dotori's browser has no place in the chat window. When it needs the owner, a card asks at the end of the conversation, showing the page as it is now, and the browser opens in a window of its own only when the owner opens it. A login it asks for is answered in the card.
- The conversation keeps `spacing.turn` between turns and `spacing.xl` around its edge.
- dotori's reply is plain text on the canvas, with no bubble. A small logo sits by the first line of each reply.
- The owner's message is a `bubble` on the right, at most four fifths of the width.
- A tool call shows as one muted line with an icon and what was done, such as "캘린더에서 이번 주 일정 3개를 찾았어요". It opens to show the details.
- A request for permission sits where dotori's reply would be, as a card.
- Everything on dotori's side of the conversation, replies, cards, results and other channels' lines, starts at the left edge of the conversation column. A result's icon sits at that edge, and what the result gave sits under it from the same edge. The copy icon under a message is drawn so the icon itself, not its button, meets that edge.
- Messages carry no time of their own. A line such as "오늘" separates days, and the time of a message shows when the pointer rests on it.
- The words of the conversation can be selected by dragging over them, across messages, by pressing a word twice for the word or three times for its line, and copied with the usual copy keys while the composer keeps the keyboard. The selection is marked in `selection`, as one band along each line, gaps between words included. Pressing anywhere else, the composer too, lets it go.
- When the pointer rests on a message, a row under it shows a copy icon, which turns into a check once copied, and then the time, in `muted`. The row keeps a few pixels from its message and more from the next, so it reads as part of its own message. A card that asks the owner has no copy action, since it shows all it asks, and its time shows under it like a message's. It does not name dotori's own tool, such as a command; what the tool does says enough.
- Messages from other channels keep their channel, place and sender on a small `muted` line above them. When the card of a call the owner decided is not shown, as in a conversation read back after dotori started again, what came of it is one 13px line with a 14px icon and no heading: a cross in `muted` for a declined call, a clock in `warn` for one that no longer waited, a small screen in `muted` with 결과 for what it gave, which shows under it, and an exclamation mark in a circle in `danger` with 오류 for one it allowed that failed, with nothing under it. Its time and copy action show under it like a message's, and the copy takes what the result shows, not what the model is told.

## Elevation & Depth

- Windows are solid on every OS: the canvas is opaque.
- Cards, fields, the composer and lists are `surface` with a soft two-layer shadow and no border. In the dark look a faint `rim` runs along their top edge.
- A control raised inside a card, such as the chosen segment of a switch, is `surface` with a smaller shadow.
- A card that carries a meaning keeps its color and takes the same soft shadow.
- Cards and fields are set apart by fill and shadow, not by borders around them.
- A small round mark, such as a theme color or the badge on the picture, never has a line around it. A mark whose fill can match what it sits on takes a soft shade all around it instead.
- Lines are used only to divide large areas.
- The composer floats lowest and carries the deepest shadow, so it reads as the place to type.

## Shapes

- Messages and cards use `rounded.lg` or `rounded.xl`. Buttons and fields inside them use `rounded.md`. The composer uses `rounded.composer`.
- The send button is round. The onboarding window's buttons and switches are pills (`rounded.full`), and its fields and rows use `rounded.field`.

## Motion & Micro-interaction

- What the owner does shows at once: a key, a click, and the owner's own message. What comes back from dotori moves in.
- A new reply, tool line or card fades in and rises by `spacing.rise` with `motion.message-enter`.
- A tool line opens and closes with `motion.expand`, its height and opacity together.
- When the pointer enters or leaves a button or a message, its color and anything that shows on hover change with `motion.hover`.
- When the conversation is at the bottom, it follows new text with `motion.follow`. When the owner has scrolled up to read, it stays where it is.
- A reply that is being written grows as its text arrives. While dotori works before the first word, the thinking mark breathes in a bubble of its own. The splash's picture breathes the same way while dotori starts. These are the only motions that repeat.
- Switching themes uses `motion.cross-fade`.
- In a switch such as the look switch or the model tabs, the raised segment slides from the old choice to the new one with `motion.switch`. The labels change at once.
- An on and off switch, such as a channel's, slides its knob to the other end and fades its `primary` fill in or out with `motion.switch`.
- What a switch changes below it fades in and rises by `spacing.rise` with `motion.message-enter`.
- A click anywhere but a field takes the focus away from the field.
- A settings link opens its section at the top and scrolls the pane down to the part it names with `motion.glide`, so the owner sees where the part sits instead of landing there at once.
- Nothing bounces, overshoots or spins without a label. Apart from `motion.working`, `motion.greet`, `motion.step` and `motion.glide`, nothing takes longer than `motion.message-enter`.
- An onboarding step comes in as one screen: it fades in and slides `spacing.slide` from the side it comes from with `motion.step`, from the right on 다음 and from the left on 이전.
- When the system asks for less motion, things change in place without moving.
- Work that waits on a disk or the network never runs on the thread that draws the window.

## Voice & Content Strategy

- The windows speak Korean or English: the language the owner chose in the settings, or else the system's. Every string is written in both, each to its own rules below, not translated word for word.
- dotori sounds like one calm, capable person: short, warm, and plain. It never pleads, gushes, apologizes for nothing or uses exclamation marks.

### Both languages

- Each fact is said once per screen. A button names its action, and nothing beside it says that again. A title, a label, a placeholder and a caption each add something the others do not; the one that only repeats goes.
- A button or link that does something on the screen is the action itself, as short as it can be, such as 저장 or Save. It names what happens, never a bare 확인 or OK when a verb fits.
- A button that is the owner's answer to dotori is said the way the owner would say it, such as 다 했어요 or I'm done.
- A sentence says what happens or what the owner can do next, such as "허락하면 모든 PC에서 지워요. 되돌릴 수 없어요".
- Say what the owner can do. A negative is for something that truly cannot happen, and then it says why or what to do instead.
- An error says what went wrong and how to fix it, in one or two short sentences, without blaming the owner.
- No line the owner does not need. A caption or a hint earns its place only by saying something the owner needs and cannot see: how to find an ID, what a choice costs. Never what the field already shows, such as the default its placeholder names (비워 두면 Dotori예요), never what is plain from using it (고르면 바로 바뀌어요), and never a fact about something else, such as the other PCs under the name and picture.
- A caption is one sentence, two at most. Anything longer becomes fewer words, not smaller text.
- One word for one thing everywhere: the assistant is called by its name, the program and its windows too, never a lowercase dotori, and a key, a token, a channel, a routine and a PC keep the same name in every window.
- A state is said in words, never by color alone.

### Korean

- 해요체 everywhere. No 합니다체, no 반말, and no "~하시겠어요?" or 시 for politeness; 시 stays only when asking about the owner's own situation.
- Active sentences with a doer: 저장했어요, not 저장되었습니다. Passive only when no one acts, such as how a key is kept: 암호화되어 보관돼요.
- Verbs over stacked Sino-Korean nouns: 키를 저장했어요, not 키 저장 완료.
- The tone is soft and friendly: every sentence ends in 요.
- Buttons that do something are short noun or verb phrases without 요, such as 저장, 다음, 키 만들기. Buttons that answer dotori end in 요, such as 다 했어요.
- The particle after the assistant's name follows the name's last sound, through `Particle` in `identity.rs`.
- Spelling and spacing follow the standard: 돼요, not 되요.

### English

- Sentence case everywhere, buttons and titles too.
- Plain words and contractions: "You can change it later."
- dotori says "I" when it speaks for itself and "you" to the owner.
- Buttons that do something start with a verb, and buttons that answer dotori are what the owner would say, such as I'm done. Buttons have no period. Sentences end with a period.
- No "please" or "sorry" as filler, and no "successfully".

## Components

- **Owner message**: `bubble` fill, `text`, `rounded.xl`, `body`, right aligned, at most four fifths of the conversation's width.
- **dotori reply**: a `surface` bubble raised a little, `rounded.xl`, `reply`, left aligned with the assistant's picture centered on the height of its first line, at most 85% of the conversation's width, lists with `primary` marks. The conversation reads like a messenger: both sides speak in bubbles, the owner's on the right in `bubble` and the assistant's on the left on `surface`.
- **Tool line**: `small`, `muted`, an icon on the left and a chevron on the right.
- **Permission card**: `warn-soft` fill, `rounded.lg`, a bold question, then on `surface` what a command does in a plain sentence above the command in `code`, and the buttons 거절 (`surface`) and 허락 (the main button). Beside the buttons only a warning that allowing cannot be undone, in `danger`; no line restates what 허락 does. Once 허락 is pressed, the buttons give way to the thinking mark, with no badge in between, and what came of it then shows in the same card; the conversation does not repeat it as a line of its own. A command's card does not show how long it may run. A result shows only what it gave, under 결과, with no line saying it went well, no badge and no exit code. A call that failed shows only an exclamation mark in a circle and 오류, small and in `danger`, and neither what the tool said went wrong nor what the command printed; dotori's answer says the rest.
- **Failure card**: `danger-soft` fill, `rounded.lg`, what failed and what to do, in `danger` and `text`.
- **Composer**: a `surface` box at `rounded.composer` with a soft shadow, straight on the canvas, a round send button that becomes the main button once there is something to send, and a `hint` line under it. While it has the keyboard it shows the `primary` outline of a focused field.
- **Browser card**: `warn-soft` fill, `rounded.lg`, what dotori asks, the page now at 16:10 on `surface` that opens the browser when clicked, and the buttons 나중에 (`surface`) and 브라우저 열기 (the main button). While the browser window is open it turns `surface` and offers 창 다시 보기 and 다 했어요.
- **Settings and window buttons**: round `surface` buttons raised a little, with a `muted` gear on the left and `muted` minimize, maximize and close marks on the right of the top strip. On macOS the gear sits on the right, since the system's buttons hold the left.
- **Settings part**: a `title` and a `caption` line under it, then its fields, on the settings pane with no fill or shadow of its own.
- **Theme list**: one 34px row per theme, its name, four 10px swatches (the light canvas and `primary`, the dark canvas and `primary`) and, on the chosen row, a `primary` check; the chosen name is semibold. The pointer lights a row with `bubble`. Under it the look switch is a `bubble` track at 11px with 시스템 따라, 밝게 and 어둡게, the chosen one raised. The settings window and the onboarding window draw the same list.
- **Field**: a `surface` card at `rounded.field`, a label above and a `caption` line under it. Only the focused field shows a `primary` outline.
- **Key field**: a masked field. ## Do's and Don'ts

- Do keep the main button for the action the owner most likely takes next, one per screen.
- Do show a state only when it needs the owner, and say it in words.
- Don't show status with colored dots.
- Don't put borders around cards or buttons. In the dark look the `rim` along a card's top edge is the one line they carry.
- Don't use gradients, glows, sparkles, emoji as icons, or cards with a colored stripe on one side.
- Don't fill large areas with `primary`, not even the owner's bubbles.
- Don't let anything jump, flicker or wait for the network before it answers the owner.
