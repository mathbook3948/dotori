# dotori

## Overview

dotori is one person's assistant that lives on their PC. Its windows should feel calm, soft and quick. The owner talks to an assistant, not to a contact in a messenger, so the conversation reads like a page the assistant writes, with the owner's words set apart.

Smoothness matters most. Nothing jumps, nothing flickers, and the window never waits on the network to answer a click or a key.

This file is the design the windows are moving to. Until a window has moved, `docs/architecture/windows.md` and the code describe how it is now, and a small fix follows them. A change that moves a window to this design updates `windows.md` in the same pull request.

## Colors

- The owner picks a theme in the settings window and every window changes at once. Each theme has a light and a dark set, chosen by the system look or by the owner.
- The themes are acorn, graphite, paper, forest and indigo. Acorn is the default: a warm off-white or a warm charcoal canvas, white or dark cards, and an amber `primary`.
- `canvas` is the window background. `surface` is anything that sits on it, such as a card or a button. A field on a card takes `canvas`. `raised` is a window's top bar and the composer area.
- `bubble` is the owner's message. It is a neutral shade of the canvas, never `primary`.
- The main button is the `text` color filled in, with `canvas` words: warm black in the light look and warm white in the dark. Each screen has at most one, for the action the owner most likely takes next.
- `primary` colors small marks only: onboarding's progress, the check by a chosen item, list bullets and a focused field's line. A mark drawn on `primary` would not stand out in every theme, so a filled mark such as a ticked checkbox takes the main button's colors. It never fills a large area or a button, and it is never text.
- `selection` is the background of selected text.
- `ok`, `warn` and `danger` say a result worked, waits for the owner, or failed. Each has a `-soft` fill for a card that carries that meaning. They are used as words, icons and fills, never as dots.
- Every color used for text reaches 4.5:1 against every background it sits on, placeholders included.
- An icon or a mark that carries meaning reaches 3:1 against what it sits on, such as `feature.icon` on each `feature` tile.
- The logo keeps its own colors, `logo.light` on a light theme and `logo.dark` on a dark one.

## Typography

- The windows write in Kakao Small Sans, bundled with the app under the SIL Open Font License, with its ligatures off. Code uses the fixed-width font each OS has.
- Kakao Small Sans has a regular and a bold weight, so a `fontWeight` of 600 draws bold.
- dotori's replies use `reply`, everything else in the conversation uses `body`, and labels and notes use `small` or `caption`. Hints under fields use `hint`.
- Headings in replies and window titles inside a window use `title`. The greeting of the onboarding window uses `display` and its other screens use `heading`. Headings are never colored.
- Korean text breaks only between words.
- Bold marks a few words that matter, such as the time of an event.

## Layout

- Every window opens at `size.window`, 9:16 like a small phone, so dotori sits beside other work instead of taking the screen. The chat and settings windows can grow and shrink down to `size.window-min`. The onboarding window keeps its size.
- The chat window is the conversation and the composer, with no header and no title bar of the OS where dotori can draw its own. A quiet strip at the top holds the settings button on the left and the close button on the right, and the owner drags the window by it. On macOS the system's window buttons stay on the left and the settings button follows them. Where the OS still draws its own title bar, the close button is left to it. Each reply carries the logo.
- The settings window has the same strip, with the title 설정 on the left and the window buttons on the right. Its cards sit on the canvas one under another, and the save button sits at the bottom with a short note beside it, on the canvas with no line above it.
- The onboarding window opens on the first run and again until the owner finishes it, at the step they left. It has one size and only the close button in its strip. It greets the owner on the first screen, then walks three steps: the API key, the name and theme, and the other places to talk. The greeting is left aligned like the rest, never a centered hero, and shows what to ask rather than how setup works. The key step asks only for what dotori cannot start without: the key for Anthropic, and the address, model and key for an OpenAI-compatible model.
- The strip shows no status. The chat window says nothing while things work. When dotori cannot be reached, one quiet line under the composer, where the Enter hint is, says so and what to do. It never names which PC answers. If dotori stops after the window saw it running, the window closes, because dotori only stops when the owner quits it or it crashes, and it never restarts itself.
- dotori's browser has no place in the chat window. When it needs the owner, a card asks at the end of the conversation, showing the page as it is now, and the browser opens in a window of its own only when the owner opens it. A login it asks for is answered in the card.
- The conversation keeps `spacing.turn` between turns and `spacing.xl` around its edge.
- dotori's reply is plain text on the canvas, with no bubble. A small logo sits by the first line of each reply.
- The owner's message is a `bubble` on the right, at most four fifths of the width.
- A tool call shows as one muted line with an icon and what was done, such as "캘린더에서 이번 주 일정 3개를 찾았어요". It opens to show the details.
- A request for permission sits where dotori's reply would be, as a card.
- Messages carry no time of their own. A line such as "오늘" separates days, and the time of a message shows when the pointer rests on it.
- When the pointer rests on a message, a copy action shows under it.
- Messages from other channels keep their channel, place and sender on a small `muted` line above them.

## Elevation & Depth

- Windows are solid on every OS: the canvas is opaque.
- Cards, fields, the composer and lists are `surface` with a soft two-layer shadow and no border. In the dark look a faint `rim` runs along their top edge.
- A control raised inside a card, such as the chosen segment of a switch, is `surface` with a smaller shadow.
- A card that carries a meaning keeps its color and takes the same soft shadow.
- Cards and fields are set apart by fill and shadow, not by borders around them.
- A small round mark, such as a theme color or the badge on the picture, never has a line around it. A mark whose fill can match what it sits on takes a soft shade all around it instead.
- Lines are used only to divide large areas.
- The composer floats lowest and carries the deepest shadow, so it reads as the place to type.

## Shapes

- Messages and cards use `rounded.lg` or `rounded.xl`. Buttons and fields inside them use `rounded.md`. The composer uses `rounded.composer`.
- The send button is round. The onboarding window's buttons and switches are pills (`rounded.full`), and its fields and rows use `rounded.field`.

## Motion & Micro-interaction

- What the owner does shows at once: a key, a click, and the owner's own message. What comes back from dotori moves in.
- A new reply, tool line or card fades in and rises by `spacing.rise` with `motion.message-enter`.
- A tool line opens and closes with `motion.expand`, its height and opacity together.
- When the pointer enters or leaves a button or a message, its color and anything that shows on hover change with `motion.hover`.
- When the conversation is at the bottom, it follows new text with `motion.follow`. When the owner has scrolled up to read, it stays where it is.
- A reply that is being written grows as its text arrives. While dotori works before the first word, the logo by the reply breathes with `motion.working` beside a short label such as "생각하는 중". This is the only motion that repeats.
- Switching themes uses `motion.cross-fade`.
- In a switch such as the look switch or the model tabs, the raised segment slides from the old choice to the new one with `motion.switch`. The labels change at once.
- An on and off switch, such as a channel's, slides its knob to the other end and fades its `primary` fill in or out with `motion.switch`.
- What a switch changes below it fades in and rises by `spacing.rise` with `motion.message-enter`.
- A click anywhere but a field takes the focus away from the field.
- Nothing bounces, overshoots or spins without a label. Apart from `motion.working`, `motion.greet` and `motion.step`, nothing takes longer than `motion.message-enter`.
- An onboarding step comes in as one screen: it fades in and slides `spacing.slide` from the side it comes from with `motion.step`, from the right on 다음 and from the left on 이전. The progress bar of the step just reached fills from its left edge in the same time, and the bar of a step left behind empties.
- When the system asks for less motion, things change in place without moving.
- Work that waits on a disk or the network never runs on the thread that draws the window.

## Voice & Content Strategy

- The windows speak Korean or English: the language the owner chose in the settings, or else the system's. Every string is written in both, each to its own rules below, not translated word for word.
- dotori sounds like one calm, capable person: short, warm, and plain. It never pleads, gushes, apologizes for nothing or uses exclamation marks.

### Both languages

- Each fact is said once per screen. A button names its action, and nothing beside it says that again. A title, a label, a placeholder and a caption each add something the others do not; the one that only repeats goes.
- A button or link that does something on the screen is the action itself, as short as it can be, such as 저장 or Save. It names what happens, never a bare 확인 or OK when a verb fits.
- A button that is the owner's answer to dotori is said the way the owner would say it, such as 다 했어요 or I'm done.
- A sentence says what happens or what the owner can do next, such as "허락하면 바로 실행해요".
- Say what the owner can do. A negative is for something that truly cannot happen, and then it says why or what to do instead.
- An error says what went wrong and how to fix it, in one or two short sentences, without blaming the owner.
- A caption is one sentence, two at most. Anything longer becomes fewer words, not smaller text.
- One word for one thing everywhere: the assistant is called by its name, and a key, a token, a channel, a routine and a PC keep the same name in every window.
- A state is said in words, never by color alone.

### Korean

- 해요체 everywhere. No 합니다체, no 반말, and no "~하시겠어요?" or 시 for politeness; 시 stays only when asking about the owner's own situation.
- Active sentences with a doer: 저장했어요, not 저장되었습니다. Passive only when no one acts, such as how a key is kept: 암호화되어 보관돼요.
- Verbs over stacked Sino-Korean nouns: 키를 저장했어요, not 키 저장 완료.
- The tone is soft and friendly: every sentence ends in 요.
- Buttons that do something are short noun or verb phrases without 요, such as 저장, 다음, 키 만들기. Buttons that answer dotori end in 요, such as 다 했어요.
- The particle after the assistant's name follows the name's last sound, through `Particle` in `identity.rs`.
- Spelling and spacing follow the standard: 돼요, not 되요.

### English

- Sentence case everywhere, buttons and titles too.
- Plain words and contractions: "You can change it later."
- dotori says "I" when it speaks for itself and "you" to the owner.
- Buttons that do something start with a verb, and buttons that answer dotori are what the owner would say, such as I'm done. Buttons have no period. Sentences end with a period.
- No "please" or "sorry" as filler, and no "successfully".

- **Settings and close buttons**: round `surface` buttons raised a little, with a `muted` gear on the left and a `muted` cross on the right of the top strip.

- **Login card**: a `surface` card in the middle of the conversation, at most four fifths of its width, `rounded.xl`, a `rim` and a soft shadow. ## Do's and Don'ts

- Do keep the main button for the action the owner most likely takes next, one per screen.
- Do show a state only when it needs the owner, and say it in words.
- Don't show status with colored dots.
- Don't put borders around cards or buttons. In the dark look the `rim` along a card's top edge is the one line they carry.
- Don't use gradients, glows, sparkles, emoji as icons, or cards with a colored stripe on one side.
- Don't draw dotori's replies in bubbles or fill large areas with `primary`.
- Don't let anything jump, flicker or wait for the network before it answers the owner.
